Early: The Cryptographic Ledger of Human Taste
The Core Vision
We have all experienced the magic of genuine cultural discovery. You stumble across a raw, unpolished YouTube video from an unknown artist, or a brilliant late-night thought from a small account on X. You like it, you leave a comment, and you move on. Months later, that artist is Rihanna, or that thread has 140 million views.

Until now, that feeling of "I was there" was ephemeral. Screenshots are easily faked, and the internet rewards performative engagement-farming over genuine taste.

Early changes this. Early is a decentralized cultural protocol that allows users to cryptographically prove they discovered and endorsed high-value content before the network algorithm caught on. It turns human taste and serendipity into a verifiable asset class—a "Curator’s Resume"—without requiring the user to change how they naturally browse the internet.

The Unique Value Proposition
Early does not measure absolute speed; it measures the Discovery Delta—the mathematical difference between the footprint of a piece of content when you interacted with it, versus the massive cultural footprint it has today. It rewards taste, not bot-like frontrunning.

How It Works: The ZK-TLS Engine
At its core, Early uses Reclaim Protocol (ZK-TLS) to bridge Web2 social silos to Web3. ZK-TLS allows a user’s browser to fetch private, authenticated data from platforms like X, YouTube, and Instagram, and generate a Zero-Knowledge Proof that the data is authentic.

Because it uses ZK-TLS, Early never sees the user’s passwords, cannot steal their cookies, and redacts all personal browsing history. It only extracts the exact cryptographic facts needed to prove the interaction.
